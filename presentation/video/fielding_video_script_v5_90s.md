# Fielding video: 90-second script (v5)

About 205 words, roughly 90 seconds at your normal pace. For a re-record; the slides stay the same, in the same order as the 2:20 cut (minus the "Later, at 171/9" scene, because the opening slide already shows 183 vs 185).

---

**[Slide: Two runs off]**
Two runs off. But fielding is where I can't tell you the whole story. I'm building CricPredict, a cricket prediction app for fans. Entertainment only, not betting.

**[Slide: Moments that swing a match]**
First ODI, India in New Zealand. Virat Kohli took a one-handed diving catch to dismiss Nathan Smith, and Rohit Sharma made a sharp run-out assist. Moments like these swing a match.

**[Slide: At 84/4, after 17 overs, then Finished 185 all out]**
At 84/4, after 17 overs, CricPredict predicted 199/7. New Zealand finished 185 all out.

**[Slide: 41.5 runs average error]**
Did fielding explain the gap? I can't prove it. In my backtest on men's ODIs the average error is 41.5 runs.

**[Slide: One hand-assigned rating per team]**
Fielding is one thing the model can't see. Today it's one hand-assigned rating per team, a reputation, not who is on the field. I think that holds accuracy back, on runs and on wickets.

**[Slide: Public data can't see most of fielding]**
Public data names a fielder only when a wicket falls, including run-outs from direct hits. It doesn't show dropped catches, runs saved by brilliant effort, or how hard a chance was.

**[Slide: The plan]**
The plan: build a first fielding model from what public data gives me, in shadow mode. If I can get ball-by-ball fielding data, rate the actual fielders. Test runs and wickets separately, on fresh matches. If it doesn't beat the current model, it doesn't ship.

**[Slide: Message me]**
If you hold fielding data, or work at a data provider, message me. Try CricPredict at cricket-score-predictor.com.
